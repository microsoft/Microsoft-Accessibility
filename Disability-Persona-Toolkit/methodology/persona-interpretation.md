# Persona Interpretation

Personas are bounded evaluation models. Treat their content as hypotheses about possible interface
barriers, not facts about every person associated with a disability or access need.

## Preserve variation

- Preserve qualifiers such as `may`, `some`, `often`, and `depending on`.
- Consider alternatives across desktop and mobile devices, speech and Braille output,
  assistive-technology configurations, residual vision, expertise, language, and task context.
- Do not compress multiple strategies into one supposedly universal behavior.
- Do not assume that one accessibility fix resolves every strategy, device, or intersectional need.
- Treat a persona with `status: stub` as incomplete. Do not derive persona-specific behavior that
  the stub does not state.

## Avoid deficit framing

Describe the mismatch between an interface and an access strategy. Do not present a person's body,
disability, unfamiliarity, or assistive-technology choice as a failure.

**Avoid:** Blind users navigate by headings, so adding headings resolves navigation.

**Prefer:** Clear headings may help people who navigate structurally, while others may use
landmarks, links, search, touch exploration, Braille, or sequential reading. Evaluate the complete
reading and focus experience supported by the available evidence.

## Bound conclusions

State findings as evidence-backed interface barriers for the selected evaluation lens. Persona
conclusions remain hypotheses to verify with relevant users and assistive technologies; they do not
replace research with the intended audience.
