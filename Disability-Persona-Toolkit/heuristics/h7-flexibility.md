# 7. Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

**Definition**: Shortcuts — hidden from novice users — can speed up the interaction for the expert user so that the design can cater to both inexperienced and experienced users.

**Accessibility Lens**: Provide multiple ways to navigate and find things — the ability to jump to sections, search, and move through structured content and interfaces efficiently. Shortcuts should work alongside assistive technology without conflict. Experienced assistive technology users rely on well-structured content and interfaces to navigate efficiently by heading, by section, or by type of element; a flat page with no structure forces everyone into novice-level sequential reading. Every unnecessary step, confirmation, or intermediate page adds disproportionate cost for users who navigate sequentially. Evaluate whether each step in a workflow contributes to the user's goal — steps that exist for system reasons rather than user reasons are efficiency traps.
