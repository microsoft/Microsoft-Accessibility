# Responsible Consideration Use

Considerations describe possible access needs and interaction barriers. They are evaluation lenses,
not evidence about any identifiable person.

## Permitted use

Use considerations to identify interface barriers, exploration strategies, and design
considerations. Report only observable properties of the evaluated interface and their likely
accessibility impact.

## Prohibited use

Do not use considerations to:

- infer whether an identifiable person has a disability;
- assign a diagnosis, identity, capability level, or required accommodation;
- rank, monitor, or classify people from behavior, preferences, errors, or assistive-technology
  signals;
- decide eligibility, employment, education, insurance, service access, or accommodations; or
- treat an access consideration as a representation of a person or disability community; or
- claim that everyone using an access strategy behaves in the same way.

For a prohibited request, do not perform the inference or decision.

## Observations and inferences

Keep interface observations separate from claims about people.

**Observed input:** An employee enlarges text to 200% and misses a control that moves off screen.

**Permitted finding:** At 200% zoom, the control moves outside the visible area. Review reflow and
keyboard reachability. The observation does not establish the employee's disability, diagnosis,
capability, or required accommodation.

**Prohibited finding:** The employee is a person with low vision and needs a screen magnifier.

**Observed input:** Someone navigates through headings using a screen reader.

**Permitted finding:** The page's heading hierarchy skips a level. Report the structural barrier,
not the person's disability or visual capability.

**Prohibited finding:** The screen-reader user is blind.

The selected `consideration` field identifies an access topic to examine, not a simulated identity
or classification of a real person. Describing a potential barrier for users of a navigation
strategy is permitted; assigning that strategy or a disability to an identifiable person is not.

When work concerns a real person, rely on that person's stated preferences and access needs. Do not
substitute evaluation-scope assumptions for individual input, user research, or an accommodation
process.

These instruction-level safeguards do not guarantee model compliance or prevent unrelated downstream
consumers from misusing independently obtained source content.
