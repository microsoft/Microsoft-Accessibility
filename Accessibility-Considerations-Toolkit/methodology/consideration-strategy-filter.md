# Access Strategy Filter

Before reporting a discoverability or visibility concern, examine the interface through the access
strategies relevant to the task. Do not infer those strategies from a disability label.

## Principle

Report an evidence-backed barrier when an in-scope strategy cannot reach, identify, or operate the
content reliably. A working alternative may resolve a concern for that strategy, but does not
establish that every access method works or that everyone will use that alternative.

## Examples

- **Magnification:** Co-location and left alignment may make controls easier to find in a small
  viewport. Verify actual visibility, panning, focus behavior, and writing direction; position alone
  is not proof of discoverability.
- **Keyboard interaction:** Verify Tab order, focus management, and activation. An early element in
  visual order is not necessarily early in keyboard focus order.
- **Screen-reader navigation:** Programmatically exposed headings may support structural navigation.
  Also examine landmarks, links, sequential reading, and dynamic announcements; a heading alone does
  not prove that the complete task is accessible.

## Application

1. Identify relevant access strategies from the consideration document and task evidence.
2. Verify how each strategy reaches, identifies, and operates the element.
3. If an apparent barrier is resolved by an available alternative, state the scope of that result
   instead of assuming every person can use that alternative.
4. Report remaining barriers with evidence, affected strategies, and any uncertainty.

This filter reduces speculative findings without suppressing barriers experienced through different
strategies. Verify conclusions with relevant assistive technologies and people with disabilities
rather than treating documented strategies as predictions of behavior.
