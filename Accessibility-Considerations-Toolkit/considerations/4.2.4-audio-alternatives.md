# FPS 4.2.4 — Usage without hearing

Where ICT provides auditory modes of operation, the ICT provides at least one mode of operation that
does not require hearing.

## Evaluation Considerations

Evaluate task completion without relying on audio perception: spoken dialogue, sound effects,
alerts, notification sounds, music, and other auditory information need equivalent alternatives.
This is an interaction condition, not an impersonation of a Deaf person or an assumption about
vision, movement, reading, or cognition.

Assess two distinct communication contexts without treating them as exhaustive or uniform groups:

1. **Sign language communication**: When a sign language (e.g., ASL, BSL, Auslan) is preferred or
   primary, assess captions and the availability of sign language interpretation for complex
   communication separately. Video relay services may support phone-based tasks. Captions alone do
   not establish that a sign language requirement is met.

2. **Non-signing communication**: When written/spoken language such as English is used without
   signing, assess captions and text alternatives. Lip-reading may assist when the speaker is
   visible and well-lit, but is not assumed or a replacement for captions. Sign language
   interpretation is not the selected channel in this context; do not generalise that preference to
   all Deaf people.

Both contexts require access without audio-only content or feedback. Communication language and
preferences vary; evaluate text/caption quality and sign language options as distinct requirements
rather than declaring any group fully served by one channel.

For each screenshot or interface, consider:

- Does ANY feature produce audio output? Video playback, audio alerts, notification sounds,
  background music, spoken instructions, audio CAPTCHA, audio-based tutorials?
- Are CAPTIONS available for all video and multimedia content? Are they accurate, synchronised, and
  toggleable?
- Are audio alerts and notifications ALSO conveyed visually? A sound without a banner, badge, flash,
  icon change, or other visual indicator fails this no-hearing condition.
- For COMMUNICATION features (video calls, voice chat, conferencing): are captions available? Is
  sign language interpretation supported or available as an option?
- For PHONE/IVR integrations: is there a text-based alternative channel (chat, web form, SMS, email)
  for completing the same task?
- Are there any features where audio is the SOLE information channel with no visual equivalent?

Possible barriers include video without captions, audio-only content (podcasts, audio messages) with
no transcript, notification sounds with no visual indicator, audio CAPTCHA with no visual
alternative, phone-only support with no text channel, video conferencing without live captions, and
audio-based onboarding or tutorials with no text alternative.

Keep this lens focused on audio-dependent content, status, communication, and alerts. A visual
equivalent must be available, but its presence does not prove it is perceivable or operable under
other access conditions. Screenshots cannot establish caption accuracy, synchronisation, sound
coverage, or call behaviour without further evidence.

## Assistive Technologies

### Auto-captions and live captioning

**Category**: captioning **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, Web (browser-level) **Device
context**: All

Built-in captioning systems convert speech to text in real time. Windows Live Captions, macOS Live
Captions, iOS Live Captions, Android Live Caption, and browser-level auto-captions (Chrome, Edge)
provide speech-to-text; verify availability and coverage in the relevant platform. Teams, Zoom, and
Google Meet offer their own live captioning. Check pre-recorded video, live meetings, audio
messages, and spoken notifications. Accuracy varies with audio quality, speaker clarity, accents,
and background noise; authored captions for pre-recorded content provide an accuracy advantage over
unreviewed auto-generation.

### Visual and haptic alerts

**Category**: system_alerts **Platforms**: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android **Device context**: All

System-level settings that replace or supplement audio notifications with visual cues (screen flash,
LED flash on mobile, notification banners) or haptic feedback (vibration). Windows provides Sound
Sentry (screen flash for system sounds). macOS flashes the screen for alerts. iOS and Android
support LED flash for alerts and vibration patterns. The user configures these system-wide, but they
only work for system-level events — application-specific audio cues (e.g., a custom notification
sound in a web app) may not trigger system visual alerts unless the application also provides its
own visual notification.

### Video relay services (VRS) and text relay

**Category**: relay_services **Platforms**: Cross-platform (video call or phone integration)
**Device context**: All

For Deaf sign language users who need to make phone calls or participate in voice-based
communications, video relay services provide a sign language interpreter who relays between the Deaf
user (signing via video) and the hearing party (speaking via phone). Text relay services (IP relay,
TTY relay) provide a text-based intermediary. These are relevant when the interface under evaluation
includes phone-based support, voice-based authentication, or any workflow that routes through a
phone system. The evaluation concern is whether the interface provides a direct text-based
alternative (chat, web form, email) so the user does not need to rely on relay services for routine
tasks.

## Exploration Strategies

### Scanning for caption controls on media

On encountering media, check discoverable CC/subtitle controls and caption settings for default
state, toggling, font size, colour, and position. Hidden controls can leave audio access uncertain;
a subtitle label alone does not establish complete caption coverage.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual scanning, mouse/touch interaction with player controls **Device context**:
All

**UI Requirements**: Caption controls must be visible and discoverable in the media player UI — not
buried in a settings submenu. Captions should ideally be on by default or clearly togglable. Caption
quality (accuracy, timing, speaker identification) matters — auto-generated captions without review
may have significant errors. For live content, live captioning must be available and clearly
indicated.

### Checking for visual alternatives to audio cues

Inventory notification sounds, alert tones, action feedback (e.g., message sent), and meaningful
background audio. Check each cue for a simultaneous visual banner, badge-count change, animation, or
status update; audio-only feedback is unavailable under this lens.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual scanning **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Every audio cue must have a simultaneous visual equivalent. Notification sounds
must be paired with visible notifications (banners, badges, toasts). Error sounds must be paired
with visible error indicators. Success sounds must be paired with visible confirmation. Background
audio that carries meaning (e.g., music changing to indicate a state change in a game) must have a
visual parallel.

### Verifying text channels for phone/voice features

For phone numbers, IVR, voice support, and calls, check live chat, email, web forms, SMS, or in-app
messaging for equivalent completion. Phone-only paths can block access without hearing; relay
availability does not replace evaluating a direct text path for routine tasks.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual scanning, navigation to support/contact pages **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Text-based support channels must be equally prominent alongside phone numbers.
"Contact us" pages that list only a phone number are a significant barrier. Chat and email options
should be co-located with phone options, not hidden behind additional navigation. If the interface
routes users to a phone system for critical tasks (identity verification, account recovery), a
text-based alternative must exist.

### Evaluating communication features for sign language support

Where sign language communication is relevant, check resolution, frame rate, and latency;
interpreter pinning or spotlighting; interpretation requests; and simultaneous
interpreter/shared-content views. These checks are distinct from caption availability.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual scanning, video call UI interaction **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Video quality must support sign language — low frame rates, heavy compression,
or small video tiles make sign language illegible. Video layout must allow pinning or spotlighting
of interpreter video. Side-by-side view of interpreter and shared content should be possible. For
events and webinars, sign language interpretation availability should be indicated in advance.

### Visual scanning and layout comprehension _(from shared)_

When visual scanning is available and preferred, page regions such as the header, navigation, main
content, sidebar, and footer can help establish layout. Visible control affordances and hierarchy
through headings, spacing, colour, and size may help locate actions. Do not assume that visual
scanning is available or that these cues work alone.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual perception **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Clear visual hierarchy through headings, spacing, and grouping. Interactive
elements must look interactive (visible borders, underlines, or other affordances). Primary actions
should be visually prominent.

### Mouse / pointer interaction _(from shared)_

Mouse, trackpad, or touch-pointer interaction may involve clicking, selecting text, dragging,
hovering for tooltips, or opening context menus. Evaluate the actions supported by the chosen input
method; hover and sustained dragging are not available in every pointer context.

**AT Mechanisms**: Mouse, Trackpad, Touchscreen **Device context**: Desktop

**UI Requirements**: Interactive elements are easy to activate with the intended pointer action and
are far enough apart that users do not accidentally activate adjacent controls. Hover states should
indicate interactivity. Drag-and-drop should have clear affordances.

### Reading and scrolling _(from shared)_

Visual reading may proceed sequentially or through heading scans, with scrolling to reach content
beyond the viewport. Evaluate logical flow appropriate to the writing system, such as top-to-bottom
and left-to-right in LTR content. Do not assume one reading direction or scrolling method across
languages and input configurations.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual reading, Mouse wheel / scroll gesture **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Text must be readable: sufficient contrast, reasonable font size, adequate line
spacing. Content should not require horizontal scrolling at standard viewport widths. Long pages
should provide navigation aids (table of contents, back-to-top).

### Form interaction _(from shared)_

Form tasks may involve reaching fields, typing, selecting options, checking boxes, and submitting
through different input methods. Evaluate clear labels, placeholder text that does not replace
labels, and actionable error feedback without assuming pointer access.

**AT Mechanisms**: Keyboard (typing), Mouse (clicking fields), Visual reading (labels) **Device
context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Every form field must have a visible label. Required fields must be clearly
indicated. Error messages must be specific, adjacent to the field, and visible without scrolling.
Successful submission must be clearly confirmed.

### Navigation and wayfinding _(from shared)_

Menus, breadcrumbs, links, and browser back/forward can support navigation between pages and views.
Evaluate whether the current location, available destinations, and return paths are understandable
through the access strategies in scope.

**AT Mechanisms**: Visual scanning, Mouse clicking, Browser controls **Device context**: All

**UI Requirements**: Consistent navigation across pages. Current location clearly indicated (active
nav item, breadcrumbs). Important actions reachable from any page. Browser back button should work
predictably.

### Touch / pointer interaction _(from shared)_

Touch interaction may include taps, swipes, pinch-zoom, or long presses, depending on the device and
access method. Direct touch has no hover preview; evaluate discoverable controls, target size, and
alternatives to complex gestures. Do not require every touch interaction pattern to be available to
the same person.

**AT Mechanisms**: Touchscreen tap, Swipe gestures, Pinch-zoom **Device context**: Mobile

**UI Requirements**: Touch targets are easy to activate without accidentally activating adjacent
controls — small or tightly packed targets force users to retry or hit the wrong control. No
reliance on hover for essential information. Custom gestures must have single-pointer alternatives.
Drag-and-drop must have tap-based alternatives. No accidental activation from scroll gestures.

## Interaction Contexts

### Deaf sign language user

When sign language (ASL, BSL, Auslan, etc.) is a preferred or primary communication language, assess
accurate captions and sign language interpretation separately for calls, meetings, presentations,
and education. Complex, nuanced, or real-time communication may require interpretation rather than
captions alone. VRS may support phone communication. Check legible signing video, interpreter
pinning, and advance interpretation availability without inferring how anyone thinks or processes
information.

**Primary AT**: Auto-captions and live captioning, Video relay services (VRS) and text relay
**Device context**: All **Preferred navigation**: Visual scanning and layout comprehension, Mouse /
pointer interaction, Scanning for caption controls on media

**Expectations**:

- Captions available and accurate for all video and multimedia content
- Sign language interpretation available or supportable for video conferencing and events
- Video quality sufficient for sign language communication (adequate resolution, frame rate, minimal
  latency)
- Interpreter video can be pinned, spotlighted, or viewed alongside shared content
- Text-based alternatives for all phone/voice-based features
- Visual notifications for all audio alerts and sounds
- No audio-only content without a text or sign language alternative

### Deaf non-signing user

When communication uses written/spoken language such as English without signing, check caption
availability, accuracy, and complete text alternatives. A visible, well-lit speaker may support
lip-reading where used, but cannot replace captions. Sign language interpretation is not the chosen
channel in this context; audio-only alerts, content, and phone-only support still require
alternatives.

**Primary AT**: Auto-captions and live captioning **Device context**: All **Preferred navigation**:
Visual scanning and layout comprehension, Mouse / pointer interaction, Scanning for caption controls
on media

**Expectations**:

- Captions available and accurate for all video and multimedia content
- Speaker face visible in video calls to support lip-reading
- Text-based alternatives for all phone/voice-based features
- Visual notifications for all audio alerts and sounds
- No audio-only content without a text transcript
- Auto-caption quality is high (speaker identification, punctuation, minimal errors)

### New / infrequent user _(from shared)_

First-time or infrequent interaction may require rediscovering controls and workflows. Labels,
accessible affordances, and progressive disclosure can support learning without requiring prior
knowledge of shortcuts or hidden features.

**Primary AT**: None **Device context**: All **Preferred navigation**: Visual scanning and layout
comprehension, Navigation and wayfinding, Form interaction

**Expectations**:

- Interface is learnable without documentation
- Primary actions are visually obvious
- Error messages guide toward resolution
- Navigation structure is understandable

### Expert / frequent user _(from shared)_

Frequent interaction may build familiarity with layouts, shortcuts, and workflows. Consistency and
efficient paths can support established strategies; changes may require relearning. Expertise in a
task does not establish expertise with every assistive technology.

**Primary AT**: None **Device context**: All **Preferred navigation**: Mouse / pointer interaction,
Navigation and wayfinding

**Expectations**:

- Keyboard shortcuts for common tasks
- Consistent layout across sessions and pages
- Efficient paths to frequently used features
- Changes to familiar workflows are clearly communicated

## Learning Considerations

- **Discovering caption availability**: The user must be able to determine quickly whether
  audio/video content has captions. If a video auto-plays without visible captions and no CC control
  is apparent, the user may assume the content is inaccessible and skip it — even if captions are
  available but need to be enabled. Caption controls should be visible before or as playback begins,
  not after the user has already missed content.

- **Caption quality expectations**: Check authored and auto-generated captions for accuracy, timing,
  punctuation, and speaker labels rather than assuming prior experience. Missing words or
  attribution can reduce confidence and require transcripts or written summaries as alternatives.

- **Communication feature assessment**: At call/conference entry, check live-caption availability
  and enabling before joining. Where signing is used, expose video-quality information and
  interpreter/shared-content layout options before conversation begins.

- **Phone/IVR alternative discovery**: Audio-only IVR blocks this no-hearing access condition. A
  "Call us" prompt without a co-located text option may conceal an alternative elsewhere; expose it
  alongside the phone number before the phone workflow begins.

- **Audio indicator recognition**: Check visual counterparts for message chimes, upload-completion
  sounds, and error tones. Audio-only feedback can leave an action's result uncertain and prompt
  repetition or an assumption of failure.

- **First visit** _(from shared)_: Familiar web, desktop, and mobile conventions may support
  learning, but recognition of hamburger menus, search icons, tabs, or modals is not guaranteed.
  Explain unfamiliar patterns and expose their purpose through accessible labels and feedback.

- **Mental models** _(from shared)_: Early interactions may establish expectations. Inconsistent
  behavior, such as a button that sometimes navigates and sometimes submits, can undermine
  confidence and require additional checking.

- **Error recovery** _(from shared)_: Support recovery from mistakes through undo, appropriate
  confirmation dialogs, and clear error messages. Make the consequences of irreversible actions
  clear rather than assuming every mistake can be avoided.

- **Cognitive load** _(from shared)_: Clutter, excessive options, and unclear hierarchy can increase
  attention and memory demands. Evaluate those demands in the task context without inferring a
  person's cognitive capabilities.

## Heuristic Lens Overrides

### Heuristic 1 — Visibility of System Status

Check simultaneous visual equivalents for message chimes, completion tones, and errors: banners,
progress updates, status text, or icon animation. Audio-only notifications leave status unavailable
under this lens. Live events such as "someone joined" or "recording started" must appear as text,
not only announcements.

### Heuristic 2 — Match Between System and Real World

Check "Listen to this message", "Hear the latest updates", and "Voice your opinion" for implied
audio dependence; use modality-neutral terms where possible. Podcasts, audio messages, and
voice-only notes need transcripts or text alternatives. Speaker icons or player controls alone do
not reveal caption/transcript availability.

### Heuristic 3 — User Control and Freedom

Can the user control caption behaviour? Toggle captions on/off, adjust caption styling (size,
colour, position), choose between auto-generated and authored captions? If captions auto-play over
critical UI elements, can the user reposition them? For communication features: can the user enable
captions before joining a call (not after, when they may have already missed the opening)? Can the
signing user choose their preferred video layout (interpreter pinned, side-by-side, etc.)? Audio
CAPTCHA with no visual alternative traps the user with no exit.

### Heuristic 4 — Consistency and Standards

Check consistent visual notifications and caption coverage across videos and call types (1:1, group,
webinar). Partial coverage makes feedback and content availability unpredictable; an accessible call
type does not compensate for an audio-only one.

### Heuristic 5 — Error Prevention

Audio-only error indicators (an error tone with no visible error message) prevent the user from even
knowing an error occurred, let alone preventing the next one. Error prevention for this
consideration means ensuring every error state has a clear visual representation. For forms:
validation errors that play a sound but don't visually highlight the problematic field leave the
user unaware. For communication: joining a call without confirming caption availability may waste
the user's time if captions turn out to be unavailable.

### Heuristic 6 — Recognition Rather than Recall

Caption controls, transcript links, and visual notification settings must be discoverable in context
— visible alongside the media player, within the call interface, or in notification settings — not
hidden in accessibility preferences that the user must recall how to find. If the user must remember
that a specific video has captions available and navigate to a separate settings page to enable
them, the design forces recall rather than recognition. Co-locate audio alternatives with the
audio-dependent feature.

### Heuristic 7 — Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

Assess equivalent alternatives: captions/transcripts for pre-recorded content, captions and
applicable sign language interpretation for live content, and chat/email beside phone support. A
hypothetical 5-minute phone resolution versus a 48-hour email response illustrates unequal paths,
not observed performance. Where signing is used, video-layout customisation supports efficient
interpreter visibility.

### Heuristic 8 — Aesthetic and Minimalist Design

Check whether audio players, speaker icons, and "Listen now" CTAs displace or conceal usable text
alternatives. Lead with or equally present transcripts/written summaries rather than burying them
below audio controls. Caption overlays must be readable, well-positioned, and clear of critical
content.

### Heuristic 9 — Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors

When a communication feature fails — call drops, captions stop working, video quality degrades below
sign language legibility — the error state must be conveyed visually. "Call failed" as an audio
announcement with no on-screen message leaves the user uncertain what happened. Caption outages
during a live call should be visually indicated so the user knows they're missing content, not just
experiencing a pause. Error recovery for the signing user includes: can they rejoin with the same
video layout and caption settings, or must they reconfigure?

### Heuristic 10 — Help and Documentation

Is there documentation explaining how to enable and configure captions? How to request sign language
interpretation for events? How to access text-based support channels? Help content should not be
delivered as audio or video without captions/transcripts. "Watch this tutorial video" with no
captions is a barrier in the help system itself. For communication features, documentation should
explain caption availability, sign language support options, and how to optimise video settings for
sign language use.
