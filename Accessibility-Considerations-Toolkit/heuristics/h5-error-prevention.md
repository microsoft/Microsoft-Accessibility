# 5. Error Prevention

**Definition**: Good error messages are important, but the best designs carefully prevent problems
from occurring in the first place.

**Accessibility Lens**: Provide clear instructions and constraints before input, not just after
errors occur. Required fields must be indicated in a way all users can perceive, not only through
visual cues like colour. Confirmation steps for destructive actions must be usable by all users
regardless of input method. Consider: a user filling out a long form through assistive technology —
can they understand the expected format before making a mistake, or do they only learn after
submitting? Error prevention also means preventing unnecessary physical effort: interactions that
require excessive precision, awkward simultaneous key combinations, or rapid timed responses are
error-prone for users with motor disabilities. Design interactions to tolerate imprecise input and
provide adequate time.
