# 7. Flexibility and Efficiency of Use

**Definition**: Shortcuts — hidden from novice users — can speed up the interaction for the expert
user so that the design can cater to both inexperienced and experienced users.

**Accessibility Lens**: Provide multiple ways to navigate and find things — the ability to jump to
sections, search, and move through structured content and interfaces efficiently. Shortcuts should
work alongside assistive technology without conflict. Structured content can support efficient
navigation by heading, section, or element type. A flat page may require more sequential navigation;
do not equate a selected navigation method with expertise or assume everyone uses the same
shortcuts. Every unnecessary step, confirmation, or intermediate page adds disproportionate cost for
users who navigate sequentially. Evaluate whether each step in a workflow contributes to the user's
goal — steps that exist for system reasons rather than user reasons are efficiency traps.
